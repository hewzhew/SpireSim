## Most useful conceptual corrections

1. **Do not replace archetypes with another rigid vocabulary.** The author prefers capability
   coverage to “力量战/防战” labels, but explicitly says capability language is still experience,
   not a turnkey algorithm. Our actor needs typed evidence and unknowns, not a new checklist score.
2. **Immediate strength and future structure are different axes.** A card can raise long-run access
   while lowering current numerical output. Route, HP, alternatives, and upgrade pressure decide
   whether the trade is survivable.
3. **Exhaust is a combat trajectory.** It has a startup cost, a transformation phase, and a terminal
   state. Empty Second Wind casts and indiscriminate exhaust are not evidence of an exhaust engine.
4. **Strategic and tactical choices are coupled.** Act 2 route safety, energy, zero-cost access,
   startup speed, and concrete encounter coverage must be evaluated together.
5. **HP is an option budget.** Reaper changes which fights and paths can be afforded, but only when
   its damage, timing, and access conditions are actually reachable.

## Why this should not become a score table

- The guide is a single expert's stated model, not a controlled dataset.
- The author repeatedly supplies exceptions and matchup-dependent uses.
- Its pick-rate screenshots omit Neow, shops, transforms, and other acquisition sites.
- Surviving runs and offered candidates induce selection bias.
- Community slang compresses distinct mechanics and must be normalized before comparison.

The useful artifact is therefore a hypothesis and role index. Any production change still needs a
replayable decision boundary and full-run evidence.

## Immediate project implications

- Strategic receipts should retain every offered candidate and acquisition site; otherwise future
  choice data cannot be interpreted.
- Card reward evaluation should compare capability deltas, immediate survivability, access,
  upgrade demand, duplicate marginal value, and skip—not a fixed card score.
- Combat planning should expose startup, transformation, and terminal feasibility separately.
- Boss-relic decisions should attach downsides to concrete future encounters (for example,
  Philosopher's Stone and Byrds) instead of static penalties.
- Route selection should consume the same readiness evidence as card and relic selection.

These are research directions, not authorization to change production policy.
