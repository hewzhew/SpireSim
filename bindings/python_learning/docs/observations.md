# Public observations and semantic tables

[Python interface](../README.md)

## Array layout

The control surface exposes slot identity, decision phase, ragged candidate row splits, candidate counts, V3 decision token id, public snapshot id, decision step, and an optional dense legal-action mask. Run-level `choose` accepts those same token ids plus candidate ordinals and rejects stale or cross-decision inference before decoding any row. Calling `decision_batch(semantic=True)` additionally returns the current semantic schema as five sparse, columnar NumPy table families:

- `token`: token kinds plus per-decision row splits;
- `categorical`: token index, typed field id, and categorical value;
- `scalar`: token index, typed field id, and `float32` value;
- `relation`: source token, typed relation, and target token;
- `candidate_token_indices`: direct alignment with the flattened candidate
  rows already described by `candidate_row_splits`.

`semantic_schema()` returns the numeric enum dictionaries and categorical vocabulary sizes from the same Rust definitions that produce the arrays. A trainer therefore does not need a duplicate Python feature dictionary or a source-code lookup to interpret field ids.

Use the schema returned by the installed package for numeric IDs and the current schema version.

## Strategic, combat and selection rows

Strategic rows encode run facts, context/history (including the public summary of the most recently completed combat), cards, relics, potion slots, and structured public event-option consequences such as HP/gold deltas, resource effects, random outcomes, repeatability, and combat entry, the public map graph, every `PlannerAction` variant, and typed candidate target edges. Combat rows encode the complete public learning observation: encounter and turn state, counters and ordered histories, player, monsters and intents, cards and damage projections, powers and payload cards, relics, potions, orbs, stances, and public encounter counters. Atomic candidates link directly to their hand card, potion slot, and monster target. Indexed choices also carry their reason and destination, not just the offered card or stance.

Public map rooms distinguish question-mark nodes from resolved events. Shop offer tokens include prices, affordability and purchase status even when an offer cannot currently be bought. Healing intents and potion exhaust choices retain their public amount or pending-effect evidence. Reversible card-reward reveals stay alongside navigation in the policy surface so that skipping a card reward does not force it open again.

Symbolic-selection rows retain the unchanged matching combat or strategic observation, the complete selection family/domain, the ordered chosen prefix, and the current append-or-submit candidates. Run-level multi-card domains link back to their eligible master-deck card tokens without enumerating complete combinations. Public entity collections use their declared ordered/unordered evidence. Candidate order remains fixed by its observation-local action ordinal. Internal entity ids and card UUIDs are used only to resolve relationships and never cross the bridge as feature values. An unexpected action outside the maintained surface fails encoding instead of producing a partial row.

## Public occurrence identity and card mechanics

Strategic occurrence identity is public: deck cards and card-target actions use local deck ordinals, potion targets use public slots, and no runtime card/potion UUID is available to the encoder. It retains the mechanical factorization into shared mechanics plus an identity residual. Card tokens carry definition type, rarity, upgrade-sensitive effective target/exhaust/ethereal, base and upgrade numerics, multi-damage, innate, tags, explicit mechanic-role coverage, and reviewed positive roles. Missing roles remain unknown for definition-only and partial profiles. Potion tokens carry definition rarity, class, potency, thrown status, and composable effect roles such as direct damage, block, draw, or colorless discovery. The schema declares the CardId and PotionId categorical fields whose embeddings must start at zero; an identity absent from training therefore falls back to its mechanical facts instead of injecting a random vector. Card roles and upgraded behavior are projected without physical hand indices in card and play-action features. Exact-state-equivalent starter Strike/Defend actions with the same target now share one canonical model candidate while the simulator retains every legal input. Checkpoints must match the installed semantic schema.

The graph includes per-card upgrade eligibility and owned-deck upgradeable counts, including a separate Basic-rarity count. Repeat-upgradeable cards use the core eligibility rule; Status and Curse cards are not treated as unupgraded targets. Reviewed direct self-HP-loss, on-play draw and gross energy requests retain their upgrade-adjusted definition amounts. Energy includes its delivery timing or condition, and HP loss includes its block/Intangible/Rupture attributes. These are card-owned requests, not resolved combat results: death, prevention, draw limits, hand capacity and evaluated combat modifiers still apply. Absent or variable/conditional draw profiles stay absent rather than becoming zero.

The semantic graph retains exact owned-deck size, card-type counts and persistent energy-cost buckets (0, 1, 2, 3+, X, unplayable). Counts include each owned occurrence once, retaining upgrades and permanent cost modifiers; candidates, offers and upgrade previews are excluded. They describe composition, not a damage bound, draw probability, playable hand or sufficiency judgment. Owned source cards are counted using reviewed core rules: on-play, intent-conditional, temporary and per-turn Strength are separate, as are powers that install draw or Block per later Exhaust event. Candidate cards and upgrade previews do not increase these inventory counts. Each Dark Embrace or Feel No Pain deployment also exposes its per-event request, distinct from immediate draw or Block and from active power amounts. These counts cover the reviewed card rules, not every possible relic or unreviewed mechanism.  Reviewed damage deliveries now retain amount rule, fixed/variable hit rule, target scope and play condition. Fixed counts remain requests, including their conditions; random-target hits do not promise focused damage. Per-hit signed Strength multipliers use the production Strength hook's shared query. Reviewed Strength gains carry delivery timing/condition and an upgraded magnitude parameter; Demon Form is delayed, Flex schedules a separate loss, and Spot Weakness checks the target at resolution. Strength conversion is separate from a Strength source. Missing profiles remain unknown.

## Map routes and preparation

Each map-route candidate has an exact ordinary-connection suffix summary: minimum and maximum elite, combat (Monster plus Elite), question-mark, rest and shop counts across visible continuations, plus earliest reachable distance to elite, rest and shop. Boss rooms do not count as combat rooms. The earliest shop before any elite is a joint path fact: it exists only when one continuation actually reaches that shop without first visiting an elite. The other extrema can come from different continuations and do not promise one combined path or estimate combat strength. Question-mark outcomes remain unknown and future Wing Boots flights are not assumed. The candidate-local summary gives a one-layer scorer access to route differences beyond the immediately selected node. 

Seven compact fields describe map preparation. The shared public-map owner stops each ordinary connected prefix at its first Elite, published Boss/Victory or unresolved endpoint. It retains earliest Elite distance separately for neither Rest/Shop, Rest only, Shop only and both on the same prefix; mutually exclusive branches cannot create the last class. Known Elite-free ends and unresolved leaves remain separate. At most four states per node are explored, without enumerating all complete routes. The current room's next targets supply shared `Map` facts with distance 1 for an adjacent node; route candidates include their selected node with distance 0. The current room's Rest/Shop is excluded from shared future preparation. Unknown cursors omit the projection, and zero known starts differs from missing information. Question-mark outcomes, future flights, fight damage, useful shop purchases and upgrade decisions are outside these facts.  Run-play renders route information from the same owner.

## Powers, relics and candidate effects

Public power-mechanism profiles come from the core power owner: trigger, card-type condition, effect and target, stack meaning and lifetime. Hand cards link to present on-play powers through `CardTriggersPower`; this is a conditional hook relationship, not an action recommendation or a guarantee that a card survives earlier hooks. `PowerAppliesPower` points to an effect-description token (for example Anger → Strength), never a fabricated active power instance. Real instances retain `EntityHasPower` ownership and amount. Profiles currently cover Strength, Dexterity, Weak, Vulnerable, Frail, Anger, Rage and SharpHide; missing coverage is explicit, and profiles do not replace relic interactions or execution order. PowerId joins zero-initialized identity residuals. 

`CandidateRemovesCard` represents publicly named event removals, linking each option to the exact current deck occurrence with its existing card attributes and mechanics. Resolution requires unique lineage evidence and matching public attributes; missing, stale or ambiguous targets fail encoding. Lineage values never become model features. This covers named removals such as Falling, not the complete structured event-effect vocabulary.

Rest candidates expose the actual clipped HP gain through the same engine helper as execution. It is the deterministic healing prefix; on-rest relic rewards remain separate. Each Smith candidate retains its current-card target and adds `CandidateUpgradesTo` to an `UpgradedCard` preview produced by the real upgrade operation. Current and upgraded owned-card tokens expose persistent cost and damage/block/magic parameters, qualified unconditional base outputs, upgrade-sensitive exhaust/selection roles, and combat upgrade scope. Instance growth and overrides are retained. Definition parameters remain separately named; previews do not predict complete combat damage or strategy. 

The same encoder projects four partial relic rule fields for owned relics, shop offers and reward/action payloads. Ginger and Turnip name the player debuff application prevented before Artifact; existing debuffs remain. White Beast Statue describes the 100-percent potion-drop chance override and the subsequent gate at four existing reward entries. Potion collection still uses slot/replacement and Sozu rules. Burning Blood and Black Blood expose the healing request on surviving combat victory, subject to healing restrictions and the HP cap. These fields describe rules rather than current Powers, held potions or delivered HP. Other relic effects remain unreviewed in this partial projection; missing fields are not zero-effect claims. Existing token scopes retain ownership and candidate boundaries. 

## Candidate scopes and order

`semantic.token.scope` is a `uint32` array aligned with `kind`. Zero denotes the shared current observation. Positive row-local IDs each belong to one candidate and all its payload, including upgrade previews and complete turn successor observations. Candidate IDs are unique in a row and are not model features. Row selection and concatenation retain these local scopes. Numerical candidate features omit card-reward and boss-relic `option_index`. Those indices still appear in the execution/audit contract; reordering the same offers changes addresses, not card or relic semantics. Mechanically meaningful order such as visible combat pile positions is retained. Owned cards, current inventory and pending reward descriptors remain shared; candidate payloads cannot update shared tokens or another candidate's payload.

## Rewards and act transitions

`PendingReward` tokens and `ContextHasPendingReward` retain the public reward collection at both overview and open-card-choice boundaries, with current collection-local indices. `RewardHasOfferedCard` distinguishes its card offers from owned deck cards. The collection remains present while a card reward is open. These are the existing public descriptors, not settled acquisition effects or newly inspected random outcomes; reward prices and amounts alone do not predict every relic interaction. This supplies facts for decisions across rewards without implementing inspection memory or a multi-step policy. Saved models and datasets must match their declared schema. `PendingActEntry` also retains the core's publicly completed-Boss preparation stage; current Act and upcoming Act remain separate facts.
